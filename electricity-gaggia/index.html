Electricity for the Gaggia, just enough.

Line, neutral, earth, jumpers and the three GaggiMate paths. Short, practical, for reading mid-install with the machine unplugged.

⏱ 5 min read🎧 ~3 min audio6 sources
The short version

Three things to keep

  1. FactCurrent only flows around a closed loop. A switch, a thermostat or a jumper just opens or closes that loop. S3
  2. FactEU colours: brown = Line, blue = Neutral, green/yellow = Earth. S1 S2
  3. FactGaggiMate's first safety step is: unplug the machine so nothing is live while you work. S4
First principles

Loops, switches, and the fault path

Fact A circuit is a closed loop that gives current a return path; without the loop it is an open circuit and nothing flows. S3

In the Gaggia, current leaves on Line (brown), goes through a part (heater, pump, valve), and comes back on Neutral (blue). Every switch and thermostat in between is just a gate in that loop.

Fact Earth is not the working path. It ties exposed metal to a low-impedance route so that if a live wire touches the chassis, a big fault current flows and trips the breaker/RCD or blows a fuse. S5

What "hot" meansPlain "Hot" or "live" is everyday wording for the Line conductor: the one at mains voltage relative to earth. In UK/EU terms both Line and Neutral count as live (current-carrying) conductors; earth does not. S2 Treat both brown and blue as dangerous while plugged in.
Colours

Brown, blue, green/yellow

  1. FactBrown — single AC phase (Line). IEC 60446/60445 colour scheme, which EU wiring is harmonised with. S1 S2
  2. FactBlue — Neutral. S1
  3. FactGreen/yellow — always and only the protective (earth) conductor. S1
Colour is not identity inside the machineOpinion The internal Gaggia wires (grey, white, black, the kit's red/green/yellow) are not mains-standard colours. GaggiMate has you label by position (BT.A, S.5, C.4, PMP.B…) for that reason; trust the label, not the colour. S4

Fact The cup-warmer has two grounding wires you disconnect to lift it off; GaggiMate says reconnect them on reassembly. S4 That is the earth bond for the metal chassis: don't skip it.

Spades & jumpers

Why the red BT.A–BT.B jumper exists

Each spade connector is one point in a loop. Pull it off and the loop is open.

Fact The stock brew thermostat sits in series in the heater loop. GaggiMate has you remove it and bridge BT.A and BT.B with the small red jumper, securely and insulated. S4

Reasoning Removing the thermostat would leave the loop open, so the heater could never run. The jumper restores continuity, and temperature control moves to the thermocouple + board + SSR instead. See the Gaggia briefing → jumper.

High level

Three paths — don't mix them up

1 · Heater → SSR

Fact ST.A/ST.B are extended with yellow wires to the OUTPUT side of the SSR; the yellow/black signal cable goes to its input. The SSR switches the heater only. S4 S6

2 · Pump → board P

Fact The kit's green wire goes from the pump's PMP.B position to the board's P/Pump connector. S4 The pump is not on the SSR.

3 · Valve → board V

Fact The original PMP.B lead (grey; the one not going into the pump's thermal fuse) is extended with the red wire to the board's V/Valve connector. S4

Plus the board's own feed: C.4 (brown) → L/Line via the grey kit wire; S.5 (white) → N/Neutral via the blue kit wire. S4 Where-is-what: BT / ST / PMP finder · 3D explore · companion.

Habits

Safe habits, every time

  1. FactUnplug at the wall before opening; work dry. S4
  2. OpinionDon't probe live mid-install. Test only after it's closed up, per the guide's final checks.
  3. FactNever join earth to Line or Neutral; green/yellow is exclusively protective. S1
  4. OpinionLeave mains brown/blue alone unless the guide names that exact wire (e.g. C.4, S.5).
  5. FactLabel by position, not colour, and keep a record of your wiring. S4
  6. FactInsulate every join and every removed end with tape or heatshrink. If unsure, ask a professional. S4
