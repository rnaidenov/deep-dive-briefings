The short version

Five things worth knowing

  • FACTYour C3 Pro moves 83 µm per click, and espresso sits just 7–8 clicks from zero. [T1][T2] The C3 ESP Pro moves 23 µm per click for this reason. [T3][C1]
  • FACTEvery grinder makes a target peak plus fines near 40 µm. [G1] Grinders differ mostly in how many fines they make and how wide the spread is. [G2]
  • FACTOn average, flat burrs made fewer fines than conicals. Bigger burrs didn't reliably help. [G2]
  • ESTIMATEThe first gain you'd notice is finer control and no cranking. The taste change is real, but it's more about style.
  • OPINIONA J-Ultra (~$199) fixes step size cheaply. [J1] The 078S is a strong electric flat-burr pick.
First principles

From bean to cup

1 · Beans are made of cells

Coffee cells are about 40 µm across. When beans are crushed, cracks tend to stop around that size, so fines pile up there. [G1]

2 · Burrs cut and crush

The dial sets the gap and the gap sets the target size. Alignment, tooth geometry and coating shape the spread. RPM matters too, but its effect varies by burr. [G1] In one 24-grinder dataset, RPM showed no clear link to how even the grind was. [G2]

3 · Two humps, not one

The target peak slides when you turn the dial. The fines peak stays put; only its height changes. Grinding finer makes more fines. [G1]

4 · Fines steer the water

Fines add resistance. Too many can clog the puck and cause uneven flow. [B2][P1] Extraction peaks at the finest grind that still flows evenly. [P2]

Conical vs flat, side by side

Schematic: simplified, not to scale. Conical: a cone spins inside a fixed ring and the gap narrows downward. Flat: two discs; grounds are flung out through the gap at the rim.

Interactive

How a shot forms

  1. Beans. Whole beans go in. Their cells are about 40 µm across.
  2. Burrs. Burrs cut and crush. The dial sets the gap.
  3. Particle groups. Three groups: the target peak (set by the gap), fines near 40 µm (from crushing), and a few boulders.
  4. Puck. The grounds are dosed and tamped into a bed in the basket.
  5. Flow. Water is pushed through. Fines add resistance and slow the shot; too many can make the flow uneven.
  6. Extraction. Small particles extract fast and large ones slowly. Yield peaks at the finest grind that still flows evenly.
  7. Taste. The mix sets the balance. Fewer fines tends toward clarity, more toward body (a community view, not a measurement).

Why step size matters

WHAT-IF · NOT A MEASUREMENT

Dialling in espresso mostly adjusts the amount of fines, so small steps matter. [G2] Suppose your ideal grind sits somewhere in a target window 100 µm wide.

C3 Pro20.8 µm · 1 ⚠
J-Ultra2.0 µm · 12
Stepless0.0 µm · ∞

Steps: C3 Pro 83 µm [T1], J-Ultra 8 µm [J1], stepless ≈ 0. The bar shows the average distance from ideal (s/4), or up to s/2 when one step is wider than the window. Right: clicks that fit in the window.

Worked maths: why s/4?

Say clicks sit every s µm and your ideal is equally likely anywhere between two clicks. Snap to the nearest click and the error runs from 0 (on a click) to s/2 (dead centre), rising evenly.

average error = ½ × (s/2) = s/4

C3 Pro: 83/4 ≈ 21 µm. J-Ultra: 8/4 = 2 µm. Stepless: ≈ 0, in principle. This assumes the window is at least one step wide.

🧠 Practice question

Your target window is 60 µm wide. How many C3 Pro clicks fit inside it? And what is the J-Ultra's average error?

Show the answer

60 ÷ 83 < 1, so no C3 Pro click is guaranteed to land in the window. The nearest click can be up to 41.5 µm (s/2) away. J-Ultra: 60 ÷ 8 → 7 clicks fit, with an average error of 8/4 = 2 µm.

Daily-life details

Retention: coffee left inside goes stale and ends up in the next shot. [N1] Static: a water spritz on the beans largely fixed clinging on the 078S in one review. [S4] Wear: worn burrs crush more, making more fines and boulders. [B1]
In the cup

Fact, estimate, opinion

  • FACTLow-fines shots are pulled differently and taste different. [B2][G1]
  • FACTLow-fines grinders may need setting so fine that the puck holds more fines per gram ("over-shooting"). [G2]
  • FACTKaffeemacher's tasters preferred middle-of-the-road grinders, not the most unimodal. [G2]
  • FACTA reviewer found the 078S sweet and balanced. It needs burr seasoning, and its espresso range is narrow. [S1]
  • ESTIMATEOn the C3 Pro you may be stuck between "a bit fast" and "a bit slow". Finer steps fix that first.
  • OPINIONA community view: flats lean to clarity, conicals to body. See Hoffmann's video. [V1]
