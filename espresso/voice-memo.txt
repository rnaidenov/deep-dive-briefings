Rado, here's the ninety-second version of espresso.

One. Extraction is measurable. Extraction yield equals the drink's strength times its weight, divided by the dose. Eighteen grams in, thirty-six out, at ten per cent strength, is twenty per cent.

Two. Pressure only exists because the puck resists. Flow equals pressure divided by resistance. Grind coarse, or let a channel open, and the pressure sags. Nine bar is a convention with a reason behind it: flow tends to peak there.

Three. The enemy is uneven flow. Cameron and colleagues found that finer grinding helps only down to a critical size. Past that, yield drops and shots get erratic. So go for the finest grind that still flows evenly.

For the Gaggia: flush and preheat. If it's stock, use a consistent temperature routine. Stir the grounds with a needle at full depth, tamp level, and weigh both the dose and the output. Start at one to two, and adjust the grind by taste.

GaggiMate adds PID temperature control and brew by weight. The Pro version also adds pressure and flow profiles. Installing it means rewiring at mains voltage, so treat that seriously.
