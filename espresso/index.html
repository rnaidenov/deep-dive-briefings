Espresso, from the puck up.

What the machine is really doing to the water, and how flushing, temperature, preinfusion, puck prep and a GaggiMate upgrade fit into a repeatable workflow. Written for a home Gaggia Classic Pro and a Timemore C3 Pro.

⏱ 15 min read🎧 ~11 min audio26 sources
The short version

Three things worth knowing

  1. FactExtraction can be put in numbers. Extraction yield is the dissolved coffee divided by the dry dose, which works out as EY = TDS × beverage mass ÷ dose. 18 g in, 36 g out at 10% TDS gives 20%. S1 S7
  2. FactPressure needs resistance. Flow = pressure drop ÷ resistance. The puck only "sees" 9 bar if it resists; a coarse or channelled puck lets the pressure sag. S2 S3
  3. Evidence + opinionUneven flow is the main enemy. Cameron et al. found that grinding finer raised yield only down to a critical size; below it, yield fell and shots varied more. Flushing, temperature routines, preinfusion and puck prep are all ways to make the water's path more even and repeatable. S1 S12

First principles

What extraction actually is

Tap, press → or scroll to build it up.

1 · A bed of porous particles

Ground coffee is a packed bed. Hot water flows through and dissolves the soluble part. Finer particles expose more surface. S1

2 · Even flow vs. channels

Model + experiment If flow were perfectly even, finer would always extract more. In practice yield peaked and then fell at fine settings, with more shot-to-shot variation, which points to uneven flow. S1

3 · Steer by mass, not time

Authors' recommendation Use the finest grind that still flows evenly, then steer with the mass of coffee and the mass of water rather than the shot time. Caveat: one café, reduced pump pressure, automated tamp at 98 N. S1
EY = (TDS × beverage mass) ÷ dose
Worked example

The numbers in one shot

Example Dose 18 g · Yield 36 g · Time 30 s · TDS 10%
Ratio = 36 ÷ 18 = 1 : 2
Average output flow = 36 g ÷ 30 s = 1.2 g/s (about 1.2 ml/s, taking espresso as roughly the density of water)
EY = 10% × 36 ÷ 18 = 20%, so a fifth of the dry coffee's mass is in the cup. S7
Pressure and resistanceAt a fixed pressure, doubling the resistance halves the flow (flow = ΔP ÷ R). With a fixed flow, doubling the resistance doubles the pressure drop. S2 Coffee ad Astra models the puck so that pressure scales with the square of the resistance change: a puck twice as resistant needs about 4× the pressure for the same flow. That is why pure flow profiles can spike. S9
Ratio vs. strengthHoffmann More water extracts more but dilutes. He starts around 1:2–1:2.2 and uses 2–3 g output changes for small corrections. Typical bands: ristretto 1:1–1:1.5, espresso 1:1.5–1:2.5, lungo 1:2.5–1:3+. S13
Optional practice problem

You dose 20 g and stop at 45 g. The refractometer reads 8.5% TDS. What are the ratio and the EY? Did you extract more or less than the 18 g → 36 g @ 10% example?

Answer: ratio 45 ÷ 20 = 1 : 2.25. EY = 8.5 × 45 ÷ 20 = 19.1%, slightly less than 20%, even though the drink is longer, because it is weaker.

Machine behaviour

Why flushing matters

~100 ml

Classic Pro E24 boiler, listed as 3.5 oz (lead-free brass, 1370 W, fully embedded heater). S21

150 ml

Gaggia: run about 150 cc through the group with the portafilter in, then wait a few minutes before the first coffee. S22

2 weeks

After more than two weeks unused, Gaggia says to run a whole tank through. S22

  • Fact Gaggia lists the causes of "coffee too cold" as: machine not heated, cold cups, portafilter not preheated (first coffee). S22
  • Fact The manual says to rinse the tank and use fresh drinking water, to wash the filters daily and to brush grounds off the shower screen regularly. S22
  • Inference So a flush does three jobs: it heats the group and portafilter metal, replaces water that has sat in the path, and rinses grounds off the screen.
  • Not covered Thermoblock machines heat water on demand and behave differently. No source on them was opened for this briefing, so no claims are made.
Machine behaviour

Temperature stability

Thermostat

Switches the element fully on or off, so the boiler swings around its set point. That is why people "temperature surf": brew at the same point in the swing each time. S23

PID

A controller that measures continuously and adjusts heater power. Coffeeblog: on a PID machine you "more or less just press the shot button". S23 GaggiMate replaces the brew thermostat with a thermocouple plus PID. S18

Thermal mass

Gaggia says the E24's brass boiler "improves both thermal stability and steaming performance". S21

A surfing routineCoffeeblog Flush until the light goes off. When it comes back on, wait ~10 s, tap steam for 3–6 s (UK 3–4 s, 110 V ~5 s), wait 3–4 s, then brew. "If it ain't broke don't fix it." S23
Machines differForum anecdote A 2013 Home-Barista thread found that routines don't transfer: one owner needed about 13 s of steam to reach ~202 °F; another fitted a microcontroller instead. S24

Fact GaggiMate's suggested settings for the Classic Pro include a temperature offset of 8. This raises the boiler-wall set point "to account for thermal loss of the water leaving the grouphead". S16

Water flow and pressure

Why 9 bar, and what profiling changes

  • Hoffmann The most plausible origin of 9 bar: at a fixed dose and grind, flow rises with pressure up to about 9 bar, then falls as the pressure compacts the puck. He calls 9 bar desirable but "hard mode" (his phrase, paraphrased from the transcript) and suggests lower pressure as an easier mode for weaker grinders or prep. S12
  • Fact Barista Hustle puts the flow plateau at around 5–7 bar in the puck, depending on grinder, machine and technique (citing Corrochano et al. 2015). S5
  • Fact Classic Pro E24: 15 bar Ulka pump, OPV calibrated to 9 bar. Gaggia's spec table lists "pre-brewing: manual". S21 S25
  • Fact Preinfusion wets the puck first: it removes dry spots, can reduce shot-to-shot variation and makes the puck more permeable, so you can grind finer. Full saturation can take 8–10 s or more. S4
  • Fact Pressure profiling follows a pressure target, so flow speeds up as the puck erodes. Flow profiling caps the flow, so pressure declines instead. S11 S6
  • Decent Flat to slightly rising flow (e.g. 1.0 → 2.0 ml/s) tastes best; declining flow is "flat, muted". Its best profiles use flow during preinfusion, then pressure, then an adaptive flow limit. S11
  • Hoffmann Big pressure swings make espresso "different… but rarely better". The big wins are full saturation and preventing channels. S12
What-if · toy model, not a simulation of your machine

Pressure profile vs. flow profile

Chart needs JavaScriptIn words: after preinfusion, a fixed-pressure shot's flow rises as the puck loses resistance, while a fixed-flow shot's pressure falls instead.

Model: resistance R falls linearly over the shot by the chosen percentage. Pressure mode: P fixed, flow = P ÷ R. Flow mode: flow fixed, P = flow × R, capped at 12 bar. Preinfusion is a linear ramp. Units are arbitrary but scaled to bar and ml/s. Based on flow = ΔP ÷ R. S2

The upgrade

What to expect from GaggiMate

An open-source controller with a touchscreen. It replaces the stock thermostat control on a Gaggia (and some other single-boiler machines). S19

Fits (official kits)

Gaggia Classic (1992–2014); Classic Pro, Evo and E24 (2019+); Rancilio Silvia V1–V6. Other machines through community guides or compatibility requests. S20 S17

Standard and Pro

Touch UI, PID, simple profiles, brew-by-weight with Bluetooth scales, grind-by-weight (Tasmota), HomeKit, Home Assistant, web UI, OTA updates, hot water. S15

Pro only

Profiles with pressure or flow control, exit conditions, phase transitions, full shot graphs, smooth "Pro Steam". The Pro kit adds a 0–1.6 MPa pressure transducer and plumbing. S15 S17

Limitations, as stated by the project
  • Standard: shot diagrams show only weight and temperature. Steam assist works, but "won't be as smooth as on Pro". S15
  • Installing it means rewiring mains-voltage connections. The guide says: "Exercise extreme caution… consult a professional technician", and to follow local electrical codes. S18
  • Housings are "required for mounting and electrical safety". S20
  • The PID values are starting points per machine and are tunable. S16
  • Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. S19

Not verified here How closely a Classic Pro's vibratory pump can actually follow a pressure or flow target once modded was not measured in any source I opened.

Puck prep

Checklist

Tick each step as you go. Your ticks stay on this device only. With JS off it is a plain list.

Workflow

Dial-in, one step at a time

  1. Fix the dose. Pick one basket and one dose (e.g. 18 g) and keep it. Hoffmann: change dose or grind for big moves. S13
  2. Fix the ratio. Start near 1:2 (18 → 36 g), weighing the output rather than judging volume. S13
  3. Grind to taste, not to a clock. A common guide is ~25–30 s including preinfusion. S23 Cameron et al. suggest ignoring time and steering by masses. S1 On the C3 Pro, see the grinder briefing.
  4. Taste and steer. Sour, thin or harsh → extract more: grind finer, or go 2–3 g longer. Hoffmann describes going from 36 to 39 g taking a shot from slightly harsh and sour to balanced (paraphrased). Bitter, dry or hollow → extract less. S13
  5. Watch for channels. If grinding finer makes shots erratic, you may be past the critical grind; step back coarser. S1
  6. Change one variable at a time and write it down: dose, out, time, taste.
The rest of the workflow

Water and cleaning

  • Fact SCA 2018 water recommendation: total hardness 50–175 ppm and alkalinity 40–75 ppm as CaCO₃, pH 6–8. Alkalinity below 40 raises the risk of corrosion. Espresso tolerates up to ~150 ppm alkalinity before buffering shows. (Coffearium's summary of the SCA handbook.) S26
  • Fact Gaggia recommends purified or bottled water and its own descaler only, "never… vinegar". The manual says to descale every 2–3 months in one place and every 2 months in another. S22
  • Fact Daily: wash the filters; if a malfunction appears, soak them in boiling water for 10 minutes. Regularly: brush the shower screen. S22
  • Not verified Detergent backflushing with a blind basket is widely recommended by retailers, but it is not in the Gaggia manual I opened. Follow your machine's own documentation.
