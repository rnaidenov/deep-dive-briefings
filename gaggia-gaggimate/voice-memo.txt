Quick one on the Gaggia. The stock machine has no computer. Two thermostats click the heater on and off, at about one hundred and seven degrees for brew and one hundred and forty five for steam, and one brew switch fires the pump and the three-way valve together. That's why temperature saws up and down, and why you can't soak the puck. GaggiMate removes the brew thermostat, adds a thermocouple and a solid-state relay so a PID can hold temperature, and gives the pump and valve their own wires, so profiles can do a gentle fill and a soak. The Pro adds a pressure sensor at the over-pressure valve, which closes the loop on pressure and flow. Add a Bluetooth scale and shots stop by weight. The big idea is one feedback loop. One honest gap: nobody publishes a measured over-pressure valve figure for the European E twenty four. And everything inside runs at two hundred and thirty volts, so the page explains it but the official guide is the one to follow.
