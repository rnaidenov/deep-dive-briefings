Rado, here's the ninety-second version of the Bulgarian presidential election.

Round one is this Sunday, the twenty-fifth of October. If nobody wins, there's a run-off on the first of November. To win outright, a ticket needs turnout above fifty per cent and more than half the valid votes. That has never happened since nineteen ninety-two, and pollsters expect turnout of around thirty-five to forty-one per cent. So a run-off is widely expected. That's an estimate, not a result.

The polls all have the same order. Iliana Iotova and Kiril Valchev are first, in the mid to high forties. Andrey Gyurov and Georgi Kandev are second, between twenty-two and thirty-one. Kostadin Kostadinov and Petar Volgin are third, around ten. Twenty-seven tickets are on the ballot.

Why it matters: the outgoing president, Rumen Radev, is now prime minister with a majority. The president's veto can be overridden with a hundred and twenty-one votes, and his party holds a hundred and thirty-one seats. So the real question is which presidential powers still act as a check: appointments, Constitutional Court referrals, and caretaker governments.
